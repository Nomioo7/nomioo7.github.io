BMI and Daily Calories: What the Numbers Mean

Updated November 18, 2026 · 6 min read

Body mass index (BMI) and daily calorie needs are two of the most searched health numbers, and two of the most misunderstood. This guide shows how each is calculated, what the results tell you, and where they stop being useful.

How BMI is calculated

BMI compares your weight with your height:

Metric: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²
Imperial: BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ height (in)²

Example 1: metric

70 kg and 1.75 m tall: 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 70 ÷ 3.0625 = 22.9

Example 2: imperial

180 lb and 5 ft 10 in (70 in): 703 × 180 ÷ (70 × 70) = 126,540 ÷ 4,900 = 25.8

Standard adult BMI ranges

BMICategory
Below 18.5Underweight
18.5 – 24.9Healthy weight
25.0 – 29.9Overweight
30.0 and aboveObesity

These ranges apply to adults. Children and teens are assessed using age- and sex-specific percentiles.

Where BMI falls short

  • It can't tell muscle from fat. Muscular athletes often score "overweight" while being lean.
  • It ignores where fat sits. Fat around the waist carries more health risk than fat elsewhere. A common rule of thumb is to keep your waist under half your height.
  • It varies by population. Some health bodies use lower cut-offs for people of Asian descent, because risk rises at lower BMI.
  • It shifts with age. Older adults naturally lose muscle, so the same BMI can mean more body fat.

Treat BMI as a quick screening number, not a diagnosis.

How many calories do you need per day?

Daily needs start with your basal metabolic rate (BMR), the energy your body uses at rest. The Mifflin-St Jeor equation is one of the most widely used estimates:

Men: BMR = 10 × kg + 6.25 × cm − 5 × age + 5
Women: BMR = 10 × kg + 6.25 × cm − 5 × age − 161

Then multiply BMR by an activity factor to get your total daily energy expenditure (TDEE):

Activity levelMultiplier
Sedentary (little exercise)1.2
Lightly active (1–3 days/week)1.375
Moderately active (3–5 days/week)1.55
Very active (6–7 days/week)1.725
Extra active (physical job + training)1.9

Worked example

A 30-year-old man, 80 kg, 180 cm, exercising 3–5 days a week:

  1. BMR = 800 + 1,125 − 150 + 5 = 1,780 kcal
  2. TDEE = 1,780 × 1.55 ≈ 2,759 kcal/day

For a 35-year-old woman, 65 kg, 165 cm and lightly active: BMR ≈ 1,345 kcal, and TDEE ≈ 1,850 kcal/day.

For gradual weight loss, many people aim for a moderate deficit of a few hundred calories below TDEE. For maintenance, aim for roughly TDEE. These are estimates, so track your weight over a few weeks and adjust.
