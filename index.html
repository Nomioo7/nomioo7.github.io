Why learn the formula at all?

A calculator gives you a number. Knowing how that number is produced tells you whether to trust it and which inputs matter most. For example, a one-point change in a mortgage rate moves your payment far more than you might guess, and compounding monthly instead of yearly adds less than most people expect.
